Frequently asked questions
Workspace and Site plans
What’s the difference between a Workspace and a Site plan?
Every site built in Webflow automatically lives inside of a Workspace.
When you sign up for Webflow, you get a free Workspace plan by default. A Workspace is a space where you stage sites — and where you can invite teammates and clients to work on sites together. Upgrading your Workspace plan gives you access to enhanced staging capabilities and advanced collaboration features.
Site plans give you the tools to build your site. When you sign up for Webflow, you automatically get a free Starter Site plan. When you need to publish, host, or unlock additional site-building features, you can upgrade that specific site to a paid Site plan.
How many Workspaces can I have?
Workspaces are designed to be a space for you to organize and build your sites, and invite people to build with you. You may have different needs for different Workspaces, so you can create as many as you need, but each Webflow customer is limited to one free Workspace.
How many paid Site plans can I have in my Workspace?
Any Workspace tier can support an unlimited number of paid Site plans. The main difference in site limits on Workspace tiers is the number of Starter sites each tier supports (Free = 2; Core = 10; Growth = Unlimited). As soon as you upgrade a Starter site to a paid Site plan, it no longer counts against your free Starter site limit.
Can I cancel my Site and/or Workspace at any time?
Yes, but you’ll still pay the remainder of the term for the plan you signed up for.
What is a Platform plan?
A Platform plan bundles a site, Workspace, add-ons, and more to help growing teams and enterprise organizations get started faster with Webflow.

How does Webflow Cloud pricing work?
Webflow Cloud provides built-in storage for web apps. Apps can be created and deployed without a Webflow site using the app project type, or added to an existing Webflow site to extend its functionality.
Usage follows Webflow’s existing site plan structure, with limits for bandwidth, requests, CPU usage, and storage. Paid plans include surge protection to handle temporary traffic spikes, and additional capacity can be added as usage grows.
