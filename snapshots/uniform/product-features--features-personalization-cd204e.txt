THE PERSONALIZATION GAP
Why are so many websites still unpersonalized?
Personalization has been available since the mid-2000s, yet most companies still don't use it.
The blockers
With Uniform
01
Performance
Every legacy personalization tool taxes page speed: a tag manager, a vendor script, and a decision the visitor has to wait for.
Decisions resolve at the edge before the page paints, so personalization costs the visitor nothing.
02
Flicker
The swap the visitor sees. Default content paints, the script catches up, and the page rewrites itself in front of them.
No client-side swap is left to hide.
03
Third-party script death
Browsers and privacy rules killed the old delivery model. Blocked scripts, capped cookies, and consent gates broke what once worked.
No third-party code runs in the visitor's browser.
04
Content velocity

Reuse the components you already published, and let Scout draft the variants your team can approve.
05
Stack integration
The data lives in five systems. The CDP knows the customer, the CMS knows the content, and neither one reaches the page.
Signals, enrichments, and CDP attributes feed one decision, without moving your data anywhere.
06
Where do I start
No map from zero to value. Every proposal opens at full 1:1 and stalls long before the first release.
Start with the context every first visit already carries: location, campaign, device, time.

FAQ
Personalization questions, answered
The questions that come up in every first meeting about edge-side personalization, stack fit, anonymous visitors, and privacy.
01
What is edge-side personalization?
Edge-side personalization means the personalization decision happens on the CDN edge before the page renders, so visitors receive the personalized version immediately: no client-side swap, no flicker, and no performance penalty. Uniform executes these decisions in milliseconds on your CDN.
02
Does Uniform personalization work with my existing CMS and CDN?
Yes. Uniform personalizes experiences composed from any source, including content in your existing CMS, and delivers decisions on your current CDN, without replatforming anything. SDKs cover the mainstream front-end frameworks, including React, Next.js, Svelte, Vue, Astro, and Angular, and decisions run on edges such as Akamai, Cloudflare, Vercel, and Netlify.
03
Can I personalize for anonymous first-time visitors?
Yes. Every first visit carries usable context: location, device, campaign, and time, all available on load at the edge. Uniform personalizes from that first anonymous paint, then deepens automatically as behavior reveals interest and intent, and connects CDP attributes when visitors become known.
04
How does Uniform handle privacy and consent?
Uniform's personalization is first-party and consent-aware by design: no third-party scripts, decisions at the edge, and data use scoped to what your consent framework allows. Behavioral profiling runs on your own property's signals rather than cross-site tracking.
05
How fast can a team launch its first personalization?
The first personalization typically ships in clicks, not sprints: pick a component, set a criterion such as location or campaign, and publish. VyStar grew personalized sessions from 4% to 82% within two months of starting, using location-based targeting as the entry point.
06
How do I measure personalization performance?
Uniform includes insights with the platform: a dashboard covering sessions, conversion, and uplift for every personalization and A/B test individually, plus audience segments and enrichment profiles built from real engagement. Every event also streams to your existing analytics, such as Google Analytics or Adobe Analytics, so results live where the business already reports.
07
Where does AI fit in personalization?
Scout, Uniform's agent, audits your current setup, proposes a personalization strategy grounded in your audience data, then executes it by configuring signals, enabling personalization, and generating variants, with humans approving what ships. It then optimizes continuously against measured uplift. AI removes both the strategy gap and the variant-production bottleneck.
